# Market Demand & Buyer Intent

## Purpose
Convert a supplied theme or a discovered opportunity into a research-grounded commercial opportunity and decide whether it passes the buyer-demand gate.

## Research hierarchy
For a supplied theme, resolve: Broad Category → Industry Niche → Specific Sub-Niche → Commercial Use Case → Visual Opportunity.

For Auto Concept, reverse the direction when necessary: discover a commercially meaningful buyer/workflow opportunity first, then resolve it into category → niche → sub-niche → use case → visual opportunity.

## Auto Concept opportunity discovery
When no theme or concept is supplied:
1. Inspect research-derived demand signals, evergreen needs, current/emerging signals, buyer workflows, asset utility, saturation, differentiation, and variation depth.
2. Generate opportunities from the underlying buyer problem and communication need rather than selecting from named examples.
3. Use the research market map and named niches as evidence/calibration, not as a fixed list of outputs.
4. Require each candidate to have a concrete buyer/use case and useful asset role.
5. Generate a fresh candidate set per session; do not repeat an identical candidate set or cycle through memorized topics unless explicitly asked.

## Demand Gate
Ask in order:
1. Who buys or uses it?
2. What communication problem do they solve?
3. Where is it used?
4. What visual function does it perform?
5. Is the need evergreen, current/emerging, or inference?
6. Is it commercially useful rather than decorative?
7. Is the representation saturated?

Return DEMAND-ALIGNED when buyer + use case + visual utility are concrete; DEMAND-WEAK when mainly decorative/unclear; DEMAND-UNCERTAIN when evidence is insufficient. Never equate alignment with guaranteed sales.

## Research-backed opportunity families
These are research findings and calibration examples, not a fixed Auto Concept menu:
Enterprise Technology & SaaS: cloud/DevOps, microservices/serverless; technical presentations, B2B marketing, investor decks.
Cybersecurity & Compliance: identity/access, Passkey/FIDO2; onboarding, security training, enterprise login explainers.
Green Energy & Sustainability: smart grid, batteries, ESG/decarbonization; sustainability reports and renewable proposals.
FinTech & Corporate Finance: cross-border B2B payments, treasury; financial reports, commercial banking interfaces, client acquisition.
Biomedical & Healthcare: teleconsultation/RPM; clinical campaigns, patient portals, regulatory documents.
Smart Logistics & Mobility: cold-chain logistics, EV fleets; logistics proposals, supply-chain dashboards, real-time tracking.

## Evergreen signals
Recurring needs include process diagrams, analytical comparisons, teamwork, project-management cycles, before/after analysis, cloud security, data protection, workplace inclusion, and self-managed health. Treat them as signals from the research, not mandatory concept outputs.

## Current-opportunity signals
Research describes 2024–2026 interest in tactile/multisensory warmth, fluid gradients/Halo Effect, authentic human interaction, Surreal Silliness, Dynamic Dimensions, 2D/3D combinations, Zero Trust, agentic workflows, Scope 3 ESG, and advanced cleantech. Treat these as dated research context, not permanent rules or fixed topics.

## Buyer intent
- Hero/banner: meaningful negative space, often 40–50% when copy is genuinely needed.
- Presentation/report: modular, neutral, adaptable components.
- Vertical social/ad: immediate comprehension, safe zones, concise 5–10 second motion when appropriate.
- Explainer/B-roll: isolated/alpha-friendly panels, dashboards, flow/data overlays.

## Evidence discipline
Do not fabricate quantitative demand, rankings, buyer search telemetry, or marketplace algorithm weights; the research identifies these as knowledge gaps.
